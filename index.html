Where the sun rises
and sets on the same shore

A private-island resort taking shape on Vatulele, an hour by sea from Fiji's Coral Coast. Thirty-three kilometres of water separate it from the nearest road, the nearest traffic light, the nearest hurry — leaving the thing the island is named for: the long gold light at either end of the day.

The island

Two horizons,
one stretch of sand

Most beaches give you half a day. Vatulele's western shelf gives you both ends of it. The island sits low and long in the water, and the reef wraps far enough around that you can stand in the same place at six in the morning and six at night and watch the sun do opposite things to the same sea.

That is the whole idea behind the name, and behind the resort. Lomanikaya is being built beside Lomanikaya Village, on the sheltered side of an island better known to the rest of Fiji for three things: the masi cloth its women have been painting by hand for centuries, the sacred red prawns that live in the tidal pools and are protected by local custom, and a quiet that visitors tend to describe in the same slightly embarrassed way — as something they had forgotten was available.

There is no bridge and no airstrip transfer queue. You come by boat, from the launch at Korolevu on the Coral Coast, and the crossing is part of it. By the time the outboards throttle back inside the reef you are already somewhere else.

On the island

Days here have a shape

Nothing is compulsory and nothing is scheduled to the minute. But the island has its own timetable — the tide, the light, when the boats go out — and the best days tend to follow it.

Dawn to dusk

First light on the eastern water, coffee on the sand, and twelve hours later the same walk in the other direction. The single thing the island does better than anywhere else, and it costs nothing.

Around Vatulele by boat

A full loop of the island with Ratu Ropate and Ratu Emori — limestone cliffs, empty beaches on the windward side, and the pools where the sacred red prawns live under local protection.

Music in the village

The musicians of Lomanikaya Village play most weeks, and visitors are guests rather than an audience. Expect guitars, close harmony, kava, and a later night than you planned.

The reef and the shallows

Warm, clear, and close enough to swim to. Snorkelling gear, kayaks and paddleboards stay on the beach, and the drop-off is a short paddle beyond the sand line.

Masi, by hand

Vatulele's masi — tapa cloth beaten from mulberry bark and painted in black and rust geometry — is among the finest in Fiji. The women who make it will show you how, at the pace it is actually done.

The long table

Dinner is served outdoors as the light goes, one table, everybody at it. Fish that was in the water this morning, produce from the island, and no seating plan worth the name.

Getting here

Four steps
and a boat

Vatulele is remote, not difficult. Most guests are on the island the same day they land in Fiji, and the last leg is the part they photograph.

  1. Fly to Nadi (NAN) Fiji's international gateway, served daily from Australia, New Zealand, the United States and Asia.
  2. Drive the Coral Coast South and east along Viti Levu's southern shore to Korolevu. We arrange the transfer with your booking.
  3. Board at the launch Boats leave from the Korolevu launch. Bags go on first, shoes come off early.
  4. Cross to Vatulele About an hour of open water, then the reef, then the beach at Lomanikaya.

Transfer times and departure points are confirmed with your booking — sea conditions set the schedule, not the other way round.
